# SEO Strategy

Build an evidence-backed plan for qualified organic discovery and conversions. Treat rankings and AI citations as possible outcomes, not promises.

## Build the plan

1. **Define the goal and baseline.** Identify the audience, offer, target markets and languages, conversion action, existing pages, and available Search Console or analytics data. Record missing data rather than inventing traffic, volume, or difficulty figures.
2. **Find the opportunities.** Group customer questions by search intent and buying stage. Review current page performance, competing results, crawl/indexing problems, and content gaps. Use `seo-keyword-research` for demand and phrasing validation; use `marketing-skills:seo-audit` for a deeper site audit when available. A keyword with demand still needs a page that satisfies its intent. Start with **striking distance**: queries where an existing page already ranks 4–20. In Search Console, export the last 28 days of queries with average position and filter to 4–20; without it, use a SERP tool for the queries the site targets. Most clicks go to the top 3, so these pages are the cheapest positions to gain, and the bottom-of-funnel ones first: alternatives, “X vs Y”, reviews, “best X for Y”, pricing, “X for [role]”, plus the home and product pages. For AI search, read Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance report where it is available: the pages Copilot cites, the grounding queries behind them classified by intent, the topics they cluster into, and the site's citation share. Read the mix, not the total: citations mostly for informational queries call for pricing, comparison, alternatives and use-case pages; citations for commercial queries with a small share call for more specific pages and third-party validation; topics where the site is barely cited are coverage gaps.
3. **Choose the smallest useful changes.** Prefer improving a relevant existing page when it can serve the intent. For a page in striking distance, compare it with the pages directly above it: answer the query in the first sentence, cover the sub-topics they cover that are true for this site, and match the title to the query; never copy their wording. For new pages, require a distinct user need and useful original information, not just another keyword variation. Consider technical fixes, page content, internal links, and distribution together. Rank work by business relevance, evidence of demand, likely impact, effort, and dependencies.
4. **Check company identity and trust where relevant.** Review the About page as a source of clear, verifiable facts for prospective customers. It may explain what the company does and for whom, meaningful differentiators, the people behind it, how it works, and practical facts such as contact details, location, or pricing when applicable. Use readable headings and semantic HTML; a table or definition list is useful only when it helps readers scan facts. Keep claims consistent with other site pages and public profiles. Add `Organization` structured data on the homepage or About page when appropriate, reflecting visible, accurate information. Include FAQs only for real customer questions. Do not invent founders, customer counts, client names, or comparisons to fill a template.
5. **Walk the path a new customer takes.** Search traffic is only worth what the site does with it. Land on the page a search would bring someone to, then sign up as a brand-new user, go through onboarding, and use the product the way a first-time customer would, until they get the outcome they came for. Report the top five things to fix, each with what you saw at that step: a promise the landing page makes that the product does not keep, a missing step without which the product is useless, dead ends, errors, and anything that makes a new user stop. Use a test account and address the owner provides, prefer a staging environment, and stop before any real payment.
6. **Measure and revisit.** For each action, state the target page or new surface, supporting evidence, expected user outcome, and a measure such as qualified clicks, signups, leads, or sales. Mark unvalidated ideas as hypotheses. Record the baseline before each change (for a ranking page, its position for the target query) and recheck once there is enough data, usually four to six weeks for rankings. For AI search, compare citations and citation share for the target topic and intent before and after the change; Bing's AI Performance report can overlay the two periods. A change that has not moved its measure is evidence about the query, not a reason to repeat the same change: the pages above may win on links, brand, or a different intent.

## Deliverable

Give a short strategy summary and a prioritized action table: **action and page**, **intent and evidence**, **why now**, **success measure**. Put the new-customer walkthrough's top five fixes alongside it when they block conversions. Separate confirmed findings from hypotheses and note data access needed for a stronger decision. Route execution to specialized skills such as `marketing-skills:copywriting` or `marketing-skills:programmatic-seo` only when that work is actually selected.

The new-customer walkthrough draws on [Noah Kagan's post](https://x.com/noahkagan/status/2105714197657014363) and the AI-search measurement on [Alex Groberman's summary](https://x.com/alexgroberman/status/2105326154827919572) of Microsoft's AI Insights, which are in early preview. The striking-distance step draws on [Rob Hoffman's Search Console method](https://x.com/robhoffman_/status/2103200798612373982); the About-page check draws on [Rob Hoffman's post](https://x.com/RobHoffman_/status/2102476527200043250), tempered by [Google's people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), [Organization markup guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization), and [AI-search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide). Google does not define an “E-E-A-T score” or require a special About-page format for AI search.
