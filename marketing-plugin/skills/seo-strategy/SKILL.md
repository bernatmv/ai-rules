# SEO Strategy

Build an evidence-backed plan for qualified organic discovery and conversions. Treat rankings and AI citations as possible outcomes, not promises.

## Build the plan

1. **Define the goal and baseline.** Identify the audience, offer, target markets and languages, conversion action, existing pages, and available Search Console or analytics data. Record missing data rather than inventing traffic, volume, or difficulty figures.
2. **Find the opportunities.** Group customer questions by search intent and buying stage. Review current page performance, competing results, crawl/indexing problems, and content gaps. Use `seo-keyword-research` for demand and phrasing validation; use `marketing-skills:seo-audit` for a deeper site audit when available. A keyword with demand still needs a page that satisfies its intent.
3. **Choose the smallest useful changes.** Prefer improving a relevant existing page when it can serve the intent. For new pages, require a distinct user need and useful original information, not just another keyword variation. Consider technical fixes, page content, internal links, and distribution together. Rank work by business relevance, evidence of demand, likely impact, effort, and dependencies.
4. **Check company identity and trust where relevant.** Review the About page as a source of clear, verifiable facts for prospective customers. It may explain what the company does and for whom, meaningful differentiators, the people behind it, how it works, and practical facts such as contact details, location, or pricing when applicable. Use readable headings and semantic HTML; a table or definition list is useful only when it helps readers scan facts. Keep claims consistent with other site pages and public profiles. Add `Organization` structured data on the homepage or About page when appropriate, reflecting visible, accurate information. Include FAQs only for real customer questions. Do not invent founders, customer counts, client names, or comparisons to fill a template.
5. **Measure and revisit.** For each action, state the target page or new surface, supporting evidence, expected user outcome, and a measure such as qualified clicks, signups, leads, or sales. Mark unvalidated ideas as hypotheses. Recheck after changes have enough data to evaluate.

## Deliverable

Give a short strategy summary and a prioritized action table: **action and page**, **intent and evidence**, **why now**, **success measure**. Separate confirmed findings from hypotheses and note data access needed for a stronger decision. Route execution to specialized skills such as `marketing-skills:copywriting` or `marketing-skills:programmatic-seo` only when that work is actually selected.

The About-page check draws on [Rob Hoffman's post](https://x.com/RobHoffman_/status/2102476527200043250), tempered by [Google's people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content), [Organization markup guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization), and [AI-search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide). Google does not define an “E-E-A-T score” or require a special About-page format for AI search.
